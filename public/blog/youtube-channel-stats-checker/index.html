Best Free YouTube Channel Stats Checkers in 2026

A practical comparison of the best free tools for checking YouTube subscriber counts, view stats, and engagement — and which one is best for niche research vs. individual channel tracking.

By StateBlade Updated August 2026 7 min read

Checking YouTube channel stats used to mean logging into YouTube Studio and hoping the data was accurate. Today there are several free tools for checking subscriber counts, view totals, and engagement data — but they serve different purposes and have different strengths.

This guide compares the best free YouTube channel stats checkers available in 2026, what each one is good at, and how to choose the right one for your needs.

What to look for in a YouTube channel stats checker

Before comparing tools, it helps to know what you're actually trying to learn. Most creators use channel stats checkers for one of two reasons:

  • Tracking your own channel: Monitoring subscriber growth, view trends, and engagement over time
  • Researching competitors and niches: Finding who's winning in a space, what their stats look like, and what keywords they're using

These are very different use cases, and different tools excel at each. A tool built for tracking your own channel (historical charts, daily sub counts) isn't necessarily good for competitive niche research — and vice versa.

1. StateBlade — best for niche and keyword research

StateBlade is a free YouTube channel stats checker designed specifically for niche research and competitor analysis. Instead of looking up a specific channel, you search by keyword or topic — and it returns a ranked list of every channel competing in that space, with live stats for each.

What it shows: Subscriber count, total views, upload frequency, channel grade (based on reach and engagement), and the exact tags top videos use — all pulled from the official YouTube Data API.

Best for: Creators who want to research a niche before starting a channel, or who want to do competitive analysis across multiple channels at once.

What it doesn't do: Historical charts or day-by-day subscriber tracking for individual channels. It's a snapshot tool, not a time-series tracker.

Cost: Free. No account required.

2. SocialBlade — best for historical tracking

SocialBlade is the original YouTube stats tracker. It's been around since 2008 and excels at showing historical subscriber and view data for individual channels — daily counts, monthly growth, estimated earnings, and grade rankings.

What it shows: Historical subscriber growth charts, daily sub counts, monthly gains/losses, estimated revenue, and a channel grade.

Best for: Tracking a specific channel's growth trajectory over time, or checking how a competitor's subscriber count has changed month-over-month.

What it doesn't do: Keyword-based niche research. You can't type "home workouts" and get a ranked list of competing channels. It's individual channel lookup only.

Cost: Free for basic stats. Paid tiers for more detailed data.

For a detailed comparison, see our StateBlade vs SocialBlade page.

3. YouTube Studio — best for your own channel

For data on your own channel, YouTube Studio is unmatched. It shows real-time views, subscriber changes, audience demographics, traffic sources, and impression click-through rate — data no third-party tool can access.

What it shows: Everything about your own channel — traffic sources, audience retention, revenue, impressions vs. clicks, demographics.

Best for: Understanding your own performance. Nothing else comes close for this use case.

What it doesn't do: Any competitor or niche research. You can only see your own channel's data.

Cost: Free (requires Google account and a YouTube channel).

Which YouTube channel stats checker should you use?

Use StateBlade if: You're researching a niche, doing competitor analysis across multiple channels, or looking for keyword/tag data to inform your content strategy. It's the only free tool that lets you find YouTube channels by keyword and compare them side-by-side.

Use SocialBlade if: You want to track how a specific channel's subscriber count has changed over time. It's the best tool for historical trend data and daily growth tracking on individual channels.

Use YouTube Studio if: You want data on your own channel. No third-party tool can replace it for understanding your own audience and content performance.

The smart approach: Use StateBlade for research and competitor analysis, then use YouTube Studio to track your own results. They complement each other rather than compete.
