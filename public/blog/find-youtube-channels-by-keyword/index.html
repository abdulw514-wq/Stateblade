The fastest way to map out every competitor in your niche using live YouTube data — and what to do with what you find.

YouTube's own search bar is designed to surface videos, not channels. It's surprisingly hard to get a clean list of channels competing in a niche using YouTube's native interface. Most creators either search manually (slow and incomplete) or pay for tools they don't need.

This guide shows you how to find YouTube channels by keyword for free — quickly, with real data, and in a way that's actually useful for making content decisions.

Why you'd want to find YouTube channels by keyword

There are a few different reasons creators search for channels by keyword:

  • Competitor research: You want to know who's already in your niche, how big they are, and what they're doing well.
  • Niche validation: Before starting a channel, you want proof that creators in this space are actually gaining subscribers.
  • Keyword discovery: You want to see what tags top channels are using so you can borrow their keyword strategy.
  • Collab research: You're looking for channels in a similar size range to collaborate with.
  • Gap analysis: You want to spot sub-topics that top channels cover less, so you can fill the gap.

Each of these use cases requires the same starting point: a ranked list of YouTube channels for a given keyword, with real stats attached.

Method 2: YouTube's native search (slow, incomplete)

YouTube's search bar has a Channels filter under the "Type" dropdown. Type your keyword, click Filters, then select Channels. This surfaces channels YouTube thinks are relevant — but the ranking is based on relevance signals, not audience size, so you don't get a clear view of who's actually biggest in the space.

You also have to click into each channel manually to get subscriber counts. For competitive research across 10+ channels, this quickly becomes tedious. It's useful for a quick sanity check but not for structured niche research.

Method 3: YouTube Data API (technical, unlimited)

If you're comfortable with APIs, you can query the YouTube Data API directly with the search.list endpoint, using type=channel and your keyword as the q parameter. This is exactly what StateBlade does under the hood — it's the same data, just without the need to write code or manage API keys.

The free tier gives you 10,000 units per day. A channel search costs around 100 units, so you can run roughly 100 searches per day on the free quota.

What to do with the channels you find

Finding the channels is the first step. Here's how to turn that list into useful intelligence:

1. Record key stats for each channel

For the top 5–10 channels in your niche keyword, note: subscriber count, total views, upload frequency, and channel age. This gives you a baseline for what success looks like in this space.

2. Calculate the views-to-subscriber ratio

Divide a channel's recent average views per video by their subscriber count. A ratio above 10% is healthy. Channels with low ratios (under 3%) have large but disengaged audiences — which means their size is less meaningful as competition.

3. Collect their video tags

In StateBlade, click any channel to see the tags from its top videos. Collect the tags from your top 3–5 competitor channels. The tags that appear most frequently are the core keywords of the niche. These become the starting point for your own keyword strategy.

4. Look for content gaps

Scroll through the top channels' video lists. What topics are they covering? More importantly — what are they NOT covering? Topics adjacent to the niche that nobody has made a definitive video about are your opportunity.

5. Identify the upload pace you need to match

If the top channels all publish 3x per week and you can only manage 1x per week, that's useful information. Either you adjust your pace, choose a lower-competition sub-niche, or accept a slower growth trajectory going in.

Finding YouTube channels by keyword: what good looks like

A healthy niche for a new creator looks like this when you search it:

  • Top channel has 50K–500K subscribers (not 5M+)
  • Multiple channels in the 10K–100K range — proof of a pathway to growth
  • Recent upload dates across most channels — the niche is active
  • View counts that don't perfectly correlate with subscriber counts — suggests algorithm is surfacing videos to new viewers

A niche to be cautious about looks like this:

  • One dominant channel with 5M+ subscribers and everyone else under 10K
  • Most channels haven't uploaded in 6+ months
  • View counts are very low relative to subscriber counts across the board
